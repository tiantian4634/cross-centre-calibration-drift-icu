**Manuscript:** *Discrimination transports, calibration does not: calibration drift in severity scoring
across 187 intensive-care units*

Prepared 2026-10-19. This supplement contains, verbatim, the analysis plans that were written **before**
the corresponding analyses were executed, together with file-system timestamps, an explicit disclosure of
one timestamp defect, and an English summary of each plan.

> **Note on language.** The original plans are reproduced **in their original language (Chinese), unaltered**,
> because they are the primary record of what was specified before what was computed. Translating them at
> this stage would break the chain between plan and result. An English summary of each plan is provided
> below so that the content is accessible to readers of the submitted manuscript. The bracketed file
> names throughout this supplement (chronology table and P0–P6 headings) are likewise the **verbatim
> file names of the public repository**, retained unaltered: file names are identifiers, not prose, and
> each name can be checked directly against the repository linked in Data Availability.

## S1.2 Disclosure of a timestamp defect (plan P2)

We disclose this rather than conceal it, because it is exactly the kind of defect this supplement exists
to surface.

**What happened.** The content of `学习曲线_预注册.md` is generated programmatically from the module
docstring of `analysis_learning_curve.py`. That docstring has **never been edited**. However, a caching
feature was later added to the script for development speed, and the script **rewrites the plan file on
every run**. This refreshed the file's modification time to **2026-10-19 11:16:14**, which is *later* than
the output it governs (`learning_curve_raw.csv`, 07:06:05). **The modification time of P2 is therefore no
longer admissible as evidence of precedence.**

**What still supports precedence.** The run that first produced `learning_curve_raw.csv` at 07:06:05
executed, by program order, the write of the plan file *before* entering any computation. The plan
therefore existed, in the content reproduced in this supplement, no later than 07:06:05.

**Remediation.** (i) A provenance note was appended to the plan file itself, recording the defect and the
supporting argument. (ii) The writing of every plan file was changed to be **idempotent** — an existing
plan is never overwritten — so that file timestamps can no longer be disturbed by re-runs. This was applied
to all seven analysis scripts.

## S1.3 Classification of analyses: exploratory versus confirmatory

This classification was fixed after the analyses were complete and is reported for transparency. It is
**not** a claim that the plans in S1.1 were all confirmatory.

| Class | Analyses | Basis | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exploratory / descriptive** | A, B, C, D, F, G, H | No pre-specified threshold; magnitudes estimated and described | Results reported as descriptive estimates with no hypothesis test |
| **Confirmatory (pre-specified criterion)** | E, I, J, K | Each plan stated an explicit criterion *and* an explicit instruction for the case where it was not met | E: **criterion not met** (success rate never reached 50%) — reported as such. I, J: criterion met. K: **criterion not met** (two-stage and one-stage τ² differ by ~2-fold) — reported as such |

**Two of the four pre-specified criteria were not met, and both are reported in the manuscript as negative
findings rather than being reframed post hoc.** In particular:

- Analysis E pre-specified "report the smallest m at which the success rate reaches 50%; if none is
  reached, state so." The answer was *no such m exists within 200 local patients*, which is reported.
- Analysis K pre-specified agreement between two-stage and one-stage estimators of τ² (relative range
  < 25%, and within 50% of the meta-analytic median). This was **not** achieved (the multilevel estimate was
  50.1% lower than the two-stage median), and the disagreement is reported together with a proposed
  mechanism and a sensitivity analysis of the main conclusion.

The primary descriptive findings (heterogeneity magnitude, its relation to measured case-mix, its temporal
instability and its clinical consequences) are **estimates, not tests**, and are presented as such.

## S1.4 English summaries of the plans

### P0 — Feasibility probe (`路B_预注册.md`)
*Question:* does the calibration of an acute-severity score vary across individual centres?
*Design:* pooled logistic model (SOFA + age + sex) fitted on the full eICU-CRD cohort; per-centre
calibration-in-the-large offset a_h; between-centre heterogeneity by random-effects meta-analysis.
*Pre-specified criterion:* **I² > 50% AND ≥ 100 centres with ≥ 100 patients** → the research direction is
viable. *Outcome: met* (I² = 75.2%, 112 centres).

### P1 — Analyses A–D (`分析计划_预注册.md`)
*A–B:* per-centre drift quantified for SOFA, OASIS and SAPS-II, including per-centre calibration slope.
*C:* a case-mix ladder (M1 = score; M2 = + age, sex; M3 = + comorbidity, invasive ventilation) testing
whether I² falls as covariates are added. *D:* positioning of the single-centre MIMIC-IV cohort within the
eICU distribution. *Wording red line:* variation not explained by measured case-mix must be termed
**residual between-hospital variation** and must **not** be attributed to quality of care.

### P2 — Analysis E, learning curve (`学习曲线_预注册.md`)
For each centre (n ≥ 200) and each m ∈ {0, 25, 50, 100, 200}, requiring n ≥ 2m, m patients were drawn for
local recalibration and the remainder used for evaluation; each (centre, m) cell repeated 100 times.
Two strategies compared: intercept-only, and intercept plus calibration slope.
*Pre-specified criterion:* report the smallest m at which the success rate reaches 50%; if none is reached,
state so. *Outcome:* **not met** — the success rate never reached 50% within 200 patients.

**Implementation note (added 2026-10-21 during the submission-quality review).** The executed pipeline
applied the *n* ≥ 2*m* rule to all 103 centre-eligible hospitals; the centre-level filter of *n* ≥ 200
stated in the plan took effect only at *m* ≥ 100, so the cells with *m* = 0, 25 and 50 also include
44 centres with 100–199 patients. A sensitivity run restricted to centres with *n* ≥ 200 changed no
success rate by more than 1.1 percentage points (S1: *m* = 0, 21.4% → 20.3%; *m* = 25, 15.3% → 15.9%;
*m* = 50, 20.0% → 21.1%; S2 changes ≤ 0.3 points; cells with *m* ≥ 100 are identical by construction),
and the pre-specified criterion was unaffected in either setting: the success rate never reached 50%.
The reproduction script `analysis_learning_curve_filter_check.py` and its output
`learning_curve_filter_sensitivity.json` are included in the repository.

### P3 — Analyses F–H (`分析计划_第二批.md`)
*F:* split-half temporal stability — per-centre offset in 2014 versus 2015 (centres contributing ≥ 50
patients and ≥ 5 deaths in each year), reported as correlation, median |Δa| and the temporal share of
between-centre variance. *G:* stratification of heterogeneity by centre size tertiles. *H:* clinical impact —
per-centre net benefit and positive predictive value at decision thresholds 0.10–0.40.
*Wording red line:* a low split-half correlation indicates that the apparent between-centre variation
contains an unstable component; it must **not** be described as unstable quality of care.

### P4 — Analysis I, shrinkage (`分析计划_分析I.md`)
Compares four strategies — no recalibration (S0), naive intercept-only (S1), naive intercept plus slope
(S2), and **empirical-Bayes shrinkage** (S3), a_EB = ā + λ(a_MLE − ā) with λ = τ²/(τ² + SE²) — with ā and
τ² estimated by **leave-one-centre-out** cross-fitting so that no centre's evaluation data enters its own
shrinkage parameters. *Pre-specified criterion:* at m = 25 the ±10% success rate of S3 must exceed S1, and
at m ≥ 100 S3 must not be worse than S1 by more than 5 percentage points. *Outcome: met.*

### P5 — Analysis J, cross-database transfer of the shrinkage parameters (`分析计划_分析J.md`)
MIMIC-IV (8,579 patients) treated as a **single new centre**; the shrinkage target ā = +0.030 and
τ² = 0.131 were **fixed from eICU and never re-estimated on MIMIC-IV**; the true offset to recover was
a* = +0.221. *Pre-specified criterion:* at m = 25 the ±10% success rate of S3 must exceed both S1 and S0.
*Outcome: met.* *Wording red line:* MIMIC-IV is a **single-centre** database; this analysis tests transfer of
the shrinkage parameters across databases and is **not** multicentre external validation of the strategy.

### P6 — Analysis K, τ² estimation framework (`分析计划_分析K.md`)
The between-centre variance τ² re-estimated under four frameworks — DerSimonian-Laird, Paule-Mandel, REML
(two-stage random-effects meta-analysis) and a one-stage random-intercept multilevel logistic model fitted
on patient-level data. *Pre-specified criterion:* relative range of τ² across the three two-stage
estimators < 25%, and the multilevel estimate within 50% of their median. *Outcome:* first clause **met**
(range 3.9%); second clause **not met** (multilevel τ² = 0.065, i.e. 50.1% lower). Reported as a
disagreement, with a proposed mechanism (the fixed-slope two-stage estimator absorbing part of the
between-centre slope variation) and a sensitivity analysis showing that the main conclusion holds under all
three τ² values.

## S1.5 Why no prospective registry registration is claimed

This study is a **retrospective secondary analysis of already-public databases**. All analyses were
executed before this supplement was assembled, so no prospective registry entry (OSF or otherwise) can
honestly be claimed, and none is. The plans reproduced here constitute the authors' contemporaneous record
of analysis specification. For transparency we note that a registry entry made at this stage would be a
*post-hoc* registration and would carry none of the protections that prospective registration provides.

Two criteria pre-specified in this supplement (P2 and P6) were **not met**, and are reported as negative
findings in the manuscript.

## S1.6 Disclosure: a post-hoc correction to the weighting of the shrinkage target

This is disclosed rather than quietly applied, and it affects the numeric values of analyses I and J.

**What was wrong.** The empirical-Bayes shrinkage estimator is written in the plans as
`a_EB = ā + λ(a_MLE − ā)` with `λ = τ²/(τ² + SE²)`. The shrinkage intensity λ came from the
hierarchical model `a_h ~ N(ā, τ² + SE²)`, but the shrinkage target ā had been estimated with
**fixed-effect** weights (`1/SE²`), which is the estimate implied by `τ² = 0`. The two halves of the
estimator therefore rested on mutually contradictory assumptions.

**Why the corrected value is the right one.** Under the hierarchical model that λ assumes, the maximum
likelihood estimate of ā is the **random-effects weighted** mean `Σ(a_h/(SE²+τ²)) / Σ(1/(SE²+τ²))`.
The same programme already used random-effects weights for the pooled standard error `seRE` and for the
pooled mean reported in analysis K, so the fixed-effect value was additionally internally inconsistent
with quantities reported elsewhere in the same manuscript.

**Does this violate the pre-specified plans?** The plans specify *what* ā and τ² are
("estimated leave-one-centre-out" for analysis I; "fixed from eICU, never re-estimated on MIMIC-IV" for
analysis J) but **they do not specify the weighting scheme used to compute ā**. No pre-specified criterion
is changed by this correction, and no threshold is moved. The correction resolves an ambiguity in the
plans; it is not a deviation from them.

**Effect on the results.**

| Analysis J (MIMIC-IV), S3 success rate (±10%) | ā fixed-effect (original) | ā random-effects (corrected) |
|---|---|---|
| m = 25 | 53.5% | **47.0%** |
| m = 50 | 49.0% | 46.0% |
| m = 100 | 55.0% | 56.0% |
| m = 200 | 68.5% | 66.0% |
| m = 400 | 75.0% | 75.0% |

The pre-specified criterion for analysis J (at m = 25, S3 must exceed both S1 and S0) is **met under both
values** and is therefore not affected. The paired advantage over naive recalibration at m = 25 falls from
+30.0 to +23.5 percentage points (P = 6.3 × 10⁻⁷). Because the corrected success rate at m = 25 now sits
below naive recalibration at m = 200, the comparative statement about the reduction in local sample size
is reported from the corrected values throughout.

**A pre-existing inconsistency is reported for transparency.** Under the original fixed-effect value the
analyses all reported smaller standard errors of the pooled estimate than were actually used; this did not
affect any point estimate reported outside analyses I and J, and no descriptive result in analyses A–H or K
changes.

**Not changed.** All descriptive results (A–H and K) are unaffected: they depend on τ² and I², whose
estimators were already using the correct fixed-effect weights for Q. Re-running analysis A–D after the
correction reproduced **every pre-existing field exactly**, with the random-effects mean added as a new
field.

## S1.7 Specification details left open in the plans

These are recorded for completeness. Neither carries any degrees of freedom that could have been
resolved in a direction favourable to the findings, and no pre-specified threshold is affected.

| # | Left unspecified in the plan | What was done | Why this is not consequential |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Number of resamples in analysis I.** The plan for analysis I fixes the four strategies, the leave-one-centre-out design, the metrics and the criterion, but never states how many times each (centre, *m*) cell is resampled. (Analysis J's plan does state it explicitly — 200 — and the learning-curve plan states 100; both were followed.) | **200 resamples per cell** | More resamples reduce Monte-Carlo error in every arm equally. A different count would change the precision of the estimate, not its direction, and cannot move a strategy relative to the pre-specified threshold. |
| 2 | **Which paired test.** The plans for analyses I and J specify a *paired, two-sided* comparison of success rates without naming the test. | **Wilcoxon signed-rank test, two-sided** | Selected as the standard paired non-parametric test and stated as such in Methods. A paired *t*-test on a 19,678-pair difference that is plainly non-normal would be the less appropriate choice; neither test can reverse a difference of the size observed (e.g. +9.05 percentage points). |

The wording of both plans, unaltered, is reproduced in this item so that a reader can verify what was and
was not specified.
