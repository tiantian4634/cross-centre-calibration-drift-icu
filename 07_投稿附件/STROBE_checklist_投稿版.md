| **1a** | Indicate the study's design with a commonly used term in the title or the abstract. | Abstract, sentence 1 | | “In a secondary analysis of eICU-CRD (30,796 patients aged ≥65 years from 187 hospitals) we examined whether such scores discriminate and calibrate alike across centres.” |
| **1b** | Provide in the abstract an informative and balanced summary of what was done and what was found. | Abstract (200/200 words, unstructured) | | Reports the main estimate (“pooled AUC 0.732 … between-centre I² was 75.4% and the observed-to-expected mortality ratio ranged from 0.69 to 1.54, a 2.2-fold spread”), the recalibration penalty (“with 25 local patients it performed worse than no recalibration (success rate 15.5% versus 21.4%)”), and the shrinkage result (“outperforming naive recalibration at 25 patients (24.3% versus 15.5%; two-sided P = 3.7 × 10⁻¹¹²)”). Two negative findings are stated. |
| **2** | Explain the scientific background and rationale for the investigation being reported. | Introduction ¶1–3 | | “Calibration — whether the predicted probability of death matches the observed probability — is the property that determines what a risk estimate actually means at the bedside …” |
| **3** | State specific objectives, including any prespecified hypotheses. | Introduction, final ¶ | | “We quantified between-centre variation in calibration for three established severity scores, tested how much of it measured case-mix could explain, decomposed it into components attributable to centre size and to calendar year, translated it into consequences at four decision thresholds, and evaluated local recalibration as a function of the number of locally available patients — together with an empirical-Bayes shrinkage alternative.” |
| **4** | Present key elements of study design early in the paper. | Methods → Study design and setting | | “We performed a retrospective secondary analysis of two publicly available, de-identified, multicentre critical-care databases: the eICU Collaborative Research Database (eICU-CRD v2.0) and MIMIC-IV v3.1.” |
